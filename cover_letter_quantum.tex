% --- (2) 原稿の魅力 — 第1段落: 何を発見したか ---
\textbf{What we discovered.}
Catalytic quantum error correction (CQEC) is a threshold-free recovery framework whose original formulation requires the ideal target state to be known exactly.
We discovered that the target-knowledge requirement can be displaced onto a classical estimation problem: under an explicitly stated idealized recovery interface---the mode-inclusion-restricted projection of the estimate---recovery fidelity is bounded by estimation fidelity plus a mode-survival penalty, and this bound is realized in both of its regimes across an 84-condition benchmark.
The resulting design-space map is sharp: with exactly calibrated noise, channel inversion coincides with the oracle at every dimension and both are capped by a mode-survival ceiling; the noise-model-free alternative, coherence maximization, matches the ceiling to within $1.5\%$ at $d \leq 16$ and degrades with dimension; and the operational choice between the two is decided by calibration accuracy, with the dephasing rate exponentially dominant.

% --- 第2段落: なぜ重要か ---
\textbf{Why this matters.}
The result separates, for the first time in the CQEC setting, what is a classical estimation question from what is a structural limit of catalytic recovery, and it does so with the negative results reported alongside the positive ones: coherence maximization is actively harmful for mixed targets, no strategy reaches high fidelity where mode annihilation sets the ceiling, and universal single-copy recovery is impossible for any deterministic target-independent apparatus.
We believe this honest map of where blind recovery can and cannot work is the prerequisite for the two open problems the manuscript identifies---an explicit joint covariant recovery channel and an operational measurement model---which we state as future work rather than claim.

% --- 第3段落: なぜ Quantum か ---
\textbf{Why \emph{Quantum}.}
The previous round of review at \emph{Quantum} was exceptionally rigorous, and the revised manuscript is substantially the product of it: the recovery interface is now stated once and declared as unsimulated physics, the catalytic theorem is restated correctly, a general Lipschitz lemma is replaced by the rigorous one-sided projection bound that a reviewer derived, an algebraically inconsistent crossover formula is removed, every ``single-copy'' and tomographic-saturation claim is withdrawn, and all numerics are regenerated from a single forward/inverse-consistent noise implementation with raw data released.
We believe the resulting paper---narrower in claim, fully reproducible, and explicit about its scope---is well matched to \emph{Quantum}'s standards of rigor and open science, and we are grateful to the reviewers for the direction.
